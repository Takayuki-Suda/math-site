A quadratic function is a function that contains x squared. Its graph is a parabola (a U-shaped curve).

Writing it in the form y = a(x − p)² + q lets you see the vertex, axis, and how it opens at a glance.

Parabolas show up all over the natural world, like the path of a thrown ball or the arch of a bridge.
a (the coefficient) decides how the parabola "opens" and which way it "faces".

•a > 0 → opens upward (valley shape)
•a < 0 → opens downward (mountain shape)
•|a| is large → a narrow, pointed parabola
•|a| is small → a wide, spread-out parabola

Try moving only a! Keep p and q at 0.
p and q decide the position of the parabola.

•Change p → the parabola moves left or right (its shape stays the same)
•Change q → the parabola moves up or down (its shape stays the same)

Careful: when p = 2, the equation is y = a(x − 2)² + q. Watch the sign!

Fix a and move only p and q, and you'll see the parabola slide without changing shape.
The vertex is the "top (or bottom)" point of the parabola.

The vertex of y = a(x − p)² + q is (p, q).

The axis is the line of left–right symmetry of the parabola: x = p.
