Between-the-lines notes ─ Weak Mordell–Weil N1/N3

A complete proof of the Kummer pairing
The Kummer Pairing, with every step justified

We carry out the construction of the perfect pairing E(K)/mE(K) × GL/K → E[m] without skipping a single line: well-definedness, bilinearity, determining the kernels on both sides, descending to the quotients, and deriving the two injections. Includes a complete table of "where E[m] ⊂ E(K) is used".

TEXT: Silverman, AEC 2nd ed. ─ VIII.1.2 (fully expanded)

Main II Weak MW N0 Reduction lemma N1 Kummer pairing N2 Unramifiedness N3 Finiteness from field theory
Section 01

The gaps this note fills

  • ① A proof of surjectivity of [m] : E(K) → E(K) (only cited by chip in the main part)
  • ② Why "another choice can only have the form Q + T (T ∈ E[m])"
  • ③ Making explicit the action convention for the composition στ (Pστ = (Pσ)τ) ── leave this vague and the cocycle computations become uncheckable
  • ④ Handling "fixed points of an infinite Galois extension", used in determining the left kernel
  • ⑤ In determining the right kernel, the step from "the value at the chosen Q is O" to "every Q is fixed"
  • ⑥ The procedure for descending κ to the quotients E(K)/mE(K) × GL/K (each side needs checking)
  • ⑦ The general theory giving two injections δ, ρ from a perfect pairing, and a proof of both directions of "finite ⟺ finite"

Convention: throughout this note we assume E[m] ⊂ E(K) (justified by the corollary in N0). We abbreviate G := GK/K. N0's premises P1 (group structure), P2 (the action commutes with addition), P3 (fixed points), P4 (#E[m] = m²) remain in use.

Section 02

Additional premises (Q1–Q4)

Premise Q1 ─ surjectivity of [m]

[m] : E(K) → E(K) is surjective.

Open the proof (gap ①)

[m] is an isogeny of degree m²AEC III.6.2(a), in particular a nonconstant morphism of curves. A nonconstant morphism between smooth projective curves is surjective on points over an algebraically closed fieldAEC II.2.3. So for any P ∈ E(K) there is Q ∈ E(K) with [m]Q = P. The two results cited here are theorems proved in Chapters II and III of AEC, and these two citations are the entire basis for "division can always be done inside K".

Premise Q2 ─ fixed points of the absolute Galois group

E(K)G = E(K). Reason: apply the same argument as P3 in N0 with M = K, F = K (KG = K is field theory in char 0; K/K is a Galois extension).

Premise Q3 ─ the composition convention for the action (gap ③)

For the exponent notation Pσ, composition is read with the right-action convention:

Pστ := (Pσ)τ

Every cocycle-type computation from here on is checked under this convention (the same convention as AEC's exponent notation).

Premise Q4 ─ surjectivity of the restriction map (field theory)

If M/K is a (finite or infinite) Galois extension inside K, the restriction map res : G → GM/K, σ ↦ σ|M is surjective with kernel GK/M. So G/GK/M ≅ GM/K. Also, for Galois extensions (even infinite ones), [M:K] = |GM/K| (as cardinals). Reason: the isomorphism extension theorem (K-automorphisms of M extend to automorphisms of K).

Section 03 ─ (a)

Definition and well-definedness

Definition (the Kummer pairing) AEC VIII §1

For P ∈ E(K), σ ∈ G, choose Q ∈ E(K) with [m]Q = P (it exists: Q1), and

κ(P, σ) := Qσ − Q
1

The values lie in E[m]: κ(P,σ) ∈ E[m].

▸ Reason [m](Qσ − Q) = [m](Qσ) − [m]Q (P1) = ([m]Q)σ − [m]Q (P2) = Pσ − P = O (P ∈ E(K), P2).

2

Any other choice of Q has the form Q + T (T ∈ E[m]).

▸ Reason If [m]Q′ = P = [m]Q, then [m](Q′ − Q) = O (P1), i.e. T := Q′ − Q ∈ E[m] = ker[m]. This is gap ②: an application of the definition of kernel, "take the difference and it falls into the kernel".

3

The value doesn't depend on the choice: (Q+T)σ − (Q+T) = Qσ − Q.

▸ Reason (Q+T)σ = Qσ + Tσ (P2). Rearranging, the difference is Tσ − T. Since T ∈ E[m] ⊂ E(K) (assumption), Tσ = T (P2), so the difference is O. ── Where E[m] ⊂ E(K) is used [No. 1].

(a) ∎
Section 04 ─ (b)

Bilinearity

Linearity in P

4

For P₁, P₂ ∈ E(K), choosing Q₁, Q₂, Q₁ + Q₂ is a legitimate choice for P₁ + P₂.

▸ Reason [m](Q₁+Q₂) = [m]Q₁ + [m]Q₂ = P₁ + P₂ (P1).

5

κ(P₁+P₂, σ) = κ(P₁,σ) + κ(P₂,σ).

▸ Reason We showed in (a) that the value doesn't depend on the choice, so we may compute with the choice of Step 4: (Q₁+Q₂)σ − (Q₁+Q₂) = (Q₁σ − Q₁) + (Q₂σ − Q₂) (P2 and commutativity). Note that having proved well-definedness first is the logical precondition for this one line.

Linearity in σ

6

κ(P, στ) = Qστ − Q = (Qστ − Qτ) + (Qτ − Q).

▸ Reason Inserting the middle term ±Qτ (an identity in an abelian group).

7

Qστ − Qτ = (Qσ − Q)τ = κ(P,σ)τ.

▸ Reason Qστ = (Qσ)τ (the convention Q3 ── this is where the convention is really used) and (A−B)τ = Aτ − Bτ (P2).

8

κ(P,σ)τ = κ(P,σ). Therefore κ(P,στ) = κ(P,σ) + κ(P,τ).

▸ Reason κ(P,σ) ∈ E[m] ⊂ E(K) (Step 1 and the assumption), so it's fixed by τ (P2). ── Where E[m] ⊂ E(K) is used [No. 2]. Without this assumption we'd only get as far as the 1-cocycle relation κ(P,στ) = κ(P,σ)τ + κ(P,τ), not a homomorphism.

(b) ∎
Section 05 ─ (c)

Left kernel = mE(K)

9

(⊇) If P ∈ mE(K), then κ(P,σ) = O (∀σ).

▸ Reason We can write P = [m]Q₀, Q₀ ∈ E(K). By (a) we may compute with the choice Q = Q₀, and Q₀σ = Q₀ (P2), so κ(P,σ) = Q₀σ − Q₀ = O.

10

(⊆) If κ(P,σ) = O for all σ ∈ G, then P ∈ mE(K).

▸ Reason Fix one Q. By assumption Qσ = Q holds for every σ ∈ G, i.e. Q ∈ E(K)G = E(K) (Q2 ── gap ④: these are fixed points of the absolute Galois group, so the input is the field-theoretic fact that the fixed field of the infinite Galois extension K/K is K). So P = [m]Q ∈ mE(K).

(c) ∎
Section 06 ─ (d)

Right kernel = GK/L, and L/K is Galois

Definition

[m]−1E(K) := { Q ∈ E(K) : [m]Q ∈ E(K) }, L := K( x(Q), y(Q) : Q ∈ [m]−1E(K), Q ≠ O ).

Note that at this point it is unknown whether L/K is finite (knowing that is the very heart of the theorem).

11

(⊇) If σ ∈ GK/L, then κ(P,σ) = O (∀P ∈ E(K)).

▸ Reason Any legitimate choice Q for P is by definition an element of [m]−1E(K), so its coordinates lie in L. σ fixes L, so Qσ = Q.

12

(⊆) If κ(P,σ) = O for all P ∈ E(K), then σ fixes every Q ∈ [m]−1E(K).

▸ Reason Take any Q ∈ [m]−1E(K) and set P := [m]Q ∈ E(K); this Q is a legitimate choice for κ(P,σ). By (a) the value of κ doesn't depend on the choice, so O = κ(P,σ) = Qσ − Q. This is gap ⑤: "the assumption is a formula about the chosen Q, but well-definedness builds the bridge to every Q".

13

Therefore σ fixes all the generators of L, and σ ∈ GK/L.

▸ Reason If a field automorphism fixes the generators, it fixes the whole field they generate (field theory: the set of fixed elements forms a subfield).

14

L/K is a Galois extension (not necessarily finite).

▸ Reason Normality: for any σ ∈ G, Q ∈ [m]−1E(K) ⇒ [m](Qσ) = ([m]Q)σ ∈ E(K) (P2, and points of E(K) are fixed by σ), so the set [m]−1E(K) is G-stable. Hence σ preserves the set of generators of L and σ(L) = L. Any K-embedding into K is the restriction of an element of G (the extension theorem in Q4), so the image under every embedding is L, i.e. normal. Separability is char 0.

(d) ∎
Section 07

Descending to the quotients, and the two injections (gaps ⑥⑦)

15

Descent on the left: κ induces a pairing κ̄ on E(K)/mE(K) × G.

▸ Reason If P ≡ P′ (mod mE(K)) then P′ = P + [m]R (R ∈ E(K)), and κ(P′,σ) = κ(P,σ) + κ([m]R,σ) (linearity in P from (b)) = κ(P,σ) + O ((c)⊇). That is, κ(·,σ) is constant on cosets.

16

Descent on the right: κ(P,σ) depends only on σ|L. Therefore κ̄ descends to a bilinear pairing on E(K)/mE(K) × GL/K.

▸ Reason If σ|L = σ′|L, then σ′ = σι with ι := σ−1σ′ ∈ GK/L. By linearity in σ from (b), κ(P,σι) = κ(P,σ) + κ(P,ι) = κ(P,σ) + O ((d)⊇). G/GK/L ≅ GL/K is Q4 (we showed L/K is Galois in (d), so the quotient makes sense).

Results so far (the perfect pairing)

A bilinear pairing

κ̄ : E(K)/mE(K) × GL/K → E[m]

has been obtained, with both the left kernel ((c)) and the right kernel ((d)) trivial. This verifies the definition of a "perfect pairing".

17

The injection δ: δ : E(K)/mE(K) ↪ Hom(GL/K, E[m]), δ(P̄) = κ̄(P̄, ·).

▸ Reason Each δ(P̄) is a group homomorphism = linearity in σ from (b). δ itself is a homomorphism = linearity in P from (b). Injectivity: if δ(P̄) is the zero map, P is in the left kernel, and by (c) P ∈ mE(K), i.e. P̄ = 0.

18

The injection ρ: ρ : GL/K ↪ Hom(E(K)/mE(K), E[m]), ρ(σ̄) = κ̄(·, σ̄).

▸ Reason The argument symmetric to Step 17 (using triviality of the right kernel (d)).

19

Translating finiteness (⇐): if [L:K] < ∞, then E(K)/mE(K) is finite, and |E(K)/mE(K)| ≤ (m²)[L:K].

▸ Reason Hom(GL/K, E[m]) ⊆ Map(GL/K, E[m]), the latter of cardinality (m²)|GL/K| (P4 and |GL/K| = [L:K] from Q4). δ is injective (Step 17).

20

Translating finiteness (⇒): if E(K)/mE(K) is finite, then [L:K] < ∞.

▸ Reason Hom(E(K)/mE(K), E[m]) is a finite set (a subset of the maps from a finite set to a finite set). ρ is injective (Step 18), so GL/K is finite. By the last claim of Q4 (for Galois extensions [L:K] = |GL/K|), [L:K] < ∞.

∎
▸ How it's used in the proof of the theorem

The proof of the weak MW theorem actually uses only the direction of Step 19: "just show L/K is finite and you win". Showing that "finite" is the job of N2 (unramifiedness) and N3 (finiteness from field theory). Step 20 is reassurance in the other direction (that the translation is an equivalence), corresponding to the right-pointing half of the "⟺" in the main part.

If use [No. 1] disappears, κ(P,·) is no longer determined as a "map"; changing Q shifts it by a coboundary. That is, it's well defined only as a cohomology class, and δ is promoted to the connecting map E(K)/mE(K) ↪ H¹(G, E[m]). If use [No. 2] disappears, the G-action on the value group E[m] becomes nontrivial, and Hom is replaced by H¹. Each step we just followed by hand is absorbed into the general theory of exact sequences in Part 3.
