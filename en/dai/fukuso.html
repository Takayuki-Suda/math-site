2Holomorphic functions and the C-R equations
f(z)=u(x,y)+iv(x,y) is holomorphic ⟺ the Cauchy–Riemann equations hold.
∂u/∂x = ∂v/∂y and ∂u/∂y = −∂v/∂x
Examples of holomorphic functions:
f(z)=z²: u=x²−y², v=2xy → ∂u/∂x=2x=∂v/∂y ✓
f(z)=z̄ (conjugate): u=x, v=−y → ∂u/∂x=1 ≠ ∂v/∂y=−1 ✗ not holomorphic
Holomorphic functions are infinitely differentiable and have Taylor expansions (analytic).
3Cauchy's integral theorem and the residue theorem
In a holomorphic region the integral does not depend on the path.
If a singularity (pole) is inside, the value of the integral is determined by residues.

The heart of complex function theory

The complex plane ℂ
z = x+iy. Absolute value |z|=√(x²+y²), argument arg(z)=arctan(y/x).
Holomorphic functions
Complex-differentiable functions. They satisfy the C-R equations and are infinitely differentiable.
Cauchy's theorem
A closed contour integral in a holomorphic region = 0. With singularities, residues appear.
Residue theorem
∮f(z)dz = 2πi Σ Res(f, zₖ). Also useful for computing real integrals.
