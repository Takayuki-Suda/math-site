Between-the-lines notes ─ Descent and heights N3/N3

A complete proof of property (b) ─ doubling and height
Duplication quadruples the height, almost exactly

hx([2]P) ≥ 4hx(P) − C₂ ── for a lower bound, everything comes down to controlling how much cancellation can happen. The two identities the main part only described as "f₂, g₂ are also explicit polynomials of degree 3" are written out completely down to the coefficients, with a monomial-by-monomial verification table, and we finish all the way to an explicit form of the constant C₂ and a numerical check.

TEXT: Silverman, AEC 2nd ed. ─ VIII.4.2(b), VIII.4.3 (fully expanded)

Main IV Descent N0 Descent theorem N1 Foundations of height N2 Property (a) addition N3 Property (b) doubling
Section 01

The gaps this note fills

  • ① Every line of the derivation of the duplication formula x([2]P) = F(p,q)/G(p,q), and G(p,q) ≠ 0 ⟺ [2]P ≠ O
  • ② Handling the case [2]P = O ── E(ℚ)[2] has at most 4 points, so C₂ can swallow it whole
  • ③ Explicit forms of f₂, g₂ in the identities (VIII.4.3) (omitted in the main part), and a verification of the identities
  • ④ Each step of the argument δ | 4Δ′ (including the reason gcd(p⁷, q⁷) = 1)
  • ⑤ Why H(x([2]P)) = max{|F|, |G|}/δ is an equality (the exact form of reducing to lowest terms)
  • ⑥ The lower bound via the triangle inequality, and a fully explicit form of the constant C₂
  • ⑦ A numerical check on E : y² = x³ + 3 (actual values of the identities, δ and C₂)
Section 02

Fixing the premises (B1–B3)

Premise B1 ─ shape of coordinates fully proved in N1

The x-coordinate of P ≠ O can be written in lowest terms as x(P) = p/q (q = d² ≥ 1, gcd(p,q) = 1), and H(x(P)) = max{|p|, |q|} =: T ≥ 1.

Premise B2 ─ the duplication formula and 2-torsion points AEC III.2.3(d)

For P = (x, y) with y ≠ 0, x([2]P) = λ² − 2x, λ = (3x² + A)/(2y). Also, for P ≠ O, [2]P = O ⟺ y = 0 (the points where the tangent is vertical).

Premise B3 ─ basic properties of gcd elementary number theory

δ | u and δ | v ⇒ δ | (su + tv) (∀s, t ∈ ℤ). gcd(p, q) = 1 ⇒ gcd(p⁷, q⁷) = 1 (a prime dividing both p⁷ and q⁷ divides both p and q). gcd(cu, cv) = |c|·gcd(u, v).

Section 03

Homogenizing the duplication formula (gaps ①②)

Lemma (height property (b)) AEC VIII.4.2(b)

There is a constant C₂ = C₂(A, B) such that hx([2]P) ≥ 4hx(P) − C₂ for all P ∈ E(ℚ).

1

Swallowing the exceptions (gap ②): E(ℚ)[2] := { P : [2]P = O } has at most 4 points. If we take C₂ ≥ 4·max{ hx(P) : P ∈ E(ℚ)[2] }, the inequality holds automatically when [2]P = O. From now on assume [2]P ≠ O (in particular P ≠ O, y ≠ 0).

▸ Reason P ≠ O with [2]P = O has y = 0 (B2), i.e. its x-coordinate is a rational root of x³ + Ax + B = 0. A cubic has at most 3 roots, so together with O there are at most 4 points ── finitely many, so the max exists. For this C₂, h([2]P) = h(O) = 0 ≥ 4h(P) − C₂. P = O also works: 0 ≥ 0 − C₂.

2

Rewrite the duplication formula as a rational function of x:

x([2]P) = (3x² + A)²/(4y²) − 2x = [ (3x² + A)² − 8xy² ] / (4y²)
numerator = 9x⁴ + 6Ax² + A² − 8x(x³ + Ax + B) = x⁴ − 2Ax² − 8Bx + A²
denominator = 4y² = 4x³ + 4Ax + 4B

▸ Reason B2, a common denominator, and substituting y² = x³ + Ax + B (once in the numerator, once in the denominator ── here we use up the curve's equation, and from now on it's a computation in x alone). Expanding: 9x⁴ − 8x⁴ = x⁴, 6Ax² − 8Ax² = −2Ax².

3

Substitute x = p/q and multiply numerator and denominator by q⁴ to homogenize:

x([2]P) = F(p, q) / G(p, q)
F(X, Z) = X⁴ − 2AX²Z² − 8BXZ³ + A²Z⁴
G(X, Z) = 4X³Z + 4AXZ³ + 4BZ⁴

▸ Reason We just filled out the degree of each term with Z to make it homogeneous of degree 4 (x⁴ → X⁴, −2Ax² → −2AX²Z², and so on). F, G ∈ ℤ[X, Z] (A, B ∈ ℤ), so F(p,q), G(p,q) ∈ ℤ.

4

G(p, q) = 4q⁴y² > 0. In particular G(p, q) ≠ 0, and the fraction in Step 3 makes sense.

▸ Reason G(p,q) = 4q(p³ + Apq² + Bq³) = 4q·q³(x³ + Ax + B) = 4q⁴y². Positive since y ≠ 0 (Step 1) and q ≥ 1. The geometry "G vanishes ⟺ 2-torsion point" is translated into the algebra of the fraction being well defined.

Section 04

The two identities ─ explicit down to f₂, g₂ (gap ③)

Sublemma (explicit identities, complete version) AEC VIII.4.3

Set Δ′ = 4A³ + 27B². As identities in ℤ[A, B, X, Z],

f₁·F − g₁·G = 4Δ′·Z⁷, f₂·F − g₂·G = 4Δ′·X⁷

hold. Here all four are homogeneous polynomials of degree 3 in (X, Z):

f₁ = 12X²Z + 16AZ³
g₁ = 3X³ − 5AXZ² − 27BZ³
f₂ = 4Δ′X³ − 4A²BX²Z + 4A(3A³ + 22B²)XZ² + 12B(A³ + 8B²)Z³
g₂ = −A²BX³ − A(5A³ + 32B²)X²Z − 2B(13A³ + 96B²)XZ² + 3A²(A³ + 8B²)Z³

Not "there exist" but "here they are". Verifying them is a finite computation: just expand and compare. Both sides are homogeneous of degree 7 in (X, Z), so for the first identity it's enough to check the one-variable equation with Z = 1 (a homogeneous polynomial can be uniquely recovered from its dehomogenization).

Open the verification of the first identity (every line, by hand)

Set Z = 1 and expand f₁F and g₁G separately.

f₁F = (12X² + 16A)(X⁴ − 2AX² − 8BX + A²)
= 12X⁶ − 24AX⁴ − 96BX³ + 12A²X² + 16AX⁴ − 32A²X² − 128ABX + 16A³
= 12X⁶ − 8AX⁴ − 96BX³ − 20A²X² − 128ABX + 16A³

g₁G = (3X³ − 5AX − 27B)·4(X³ + AX + B)
= 4[ 3X⁶ + 3AX⁴ + 3BX³ − 5AX⁴ − 5A²X² − 5ABX − 27BX³ − 27ABX − 27B² ]
= 4[ 3X⁶ − 2AX⁴ − 24BX³ − 5A²X² − 32ABX − 27B² ]
= 12X⁶ − 8AX⁴ − 96BX³ − 20A²X² − 128ABX − 108B²

The coefficients of X⁶, X⁴, X³, X², X agree exactly and cancel in the difference, leaving only the constant term:
f₁F − g₁G = 16A³ + 108B² = 4(4A³ + 27B²) = 4Δ′. ✓

The second identity has many terms, so we give a verification table listing the coefficients of f₂F and g₂G for each monomial X7−kZk:

MonomialCoefficient in f₂FCoefficient in g₂GDifference

X⁶Z−4A²B−4A²B0
X⁵Z²−4A(5A³+32B²)−4A(5A³+32B²)0

For 7 of the 8 monomials the coefficients match exactly and cancel, leaving only 4Δ′ as the coefficient of X⁷ ── f₂F − g₂G = 4Δ′X⁷. ✓ (Expanding each coefficient is a mechanical computation of just "multiplying polynomials". For example, the coefficient of X⁶Z: on the f₂F side, the X²Z coefficient of f₂ × the X⁴ coefficient of F = −4A²B·1; on the g₂G side, the X³ coefficient of g₂ × the X³Z coefficient of G = (−A²B)·4. They agree. The other rows can be checked the same way.)

? Where do the identities come from ─ resultants and Δ²

When Δ′ ≠ 0, F(X, 1) and G(X, 1) have no common root (a common root x₀ would have y = 0 and be a common zero of the numerator and denominator of the duplication formula = a sign of a singular point). For coprime polynomials, Euclid's algorithm returns "a Bézout certificate" fF − gG = constant, and clearing denominators gives the identities above. Under the constraint of a pair of degree 3, the solution is unique, and the smallest constant that appears is 4Δ′. Incidentally, computing the resultant gives Res(F, G) = 256Δ′² = Δ² ── the nondegeneracy of the doubling map is engraved twice in the language of the discriminant. The proof of the general theorem "a morphism of degree d of projective space multiplies heights by about d"AEC VIII.5.6 is also a higher-dimensional version of this Nullstellensatz certificate.

Section 05

Controlling cancellation ─ δ | 4Δ′ (gaps ④⑤)

5

Set δ := gcd( F(p,q), G(p,q) ) ≥ 1 (defined since G(p,q) ≠ 0).

▸ Reason Step 4. gcd(u, v) (v ≠ 0) as defined in elementary number theory.

6

Substitute (X, Z) = (p, q) into the identities:

f₁(p,q)·F(p,q) − g₁(p,q)·G(p,q) = 4Δ′q⁷, f₂(p,q)·F(p,q) − g₂(p,q)·G(p,q) = 4Δ′p⁷

▸ Reason An identity in ℤ[X, Z] becomes an equation in ℤ under any integer substitution (A and B are integers fixed from the start).

7

δ | 4Δ′q⁷ and δ | 4Δ′p⁷.

▸ Reason δ divides both F(p,q) and G(p,q), so it divides their integer combinations too (the left sides in Step 6) (first property in B3, with s = f₁(p,q), t = −g₁(p,q), etc.). This is the first half of gap ④.

8

δ | 4Δ′. The amount of cancellation is capped by a constant independent of P.

▸ Reason δ | gcd(4Δ′p⁷, 4Δ′q⁷) = |4Δ′|·gcd(p⁷, q⁷) = |4Δ′| (third property in B3, and gcd(p,q) = 1 ⇒ gcd(p⁷,q⁷) = 1: second property in B3). Δ′ ≠ 0 comes from Δ = −16Δ′ ≠ 0 (nonsingularity). This is the second half of gap ④ ── the moment geometric nondegeneracy (Δ ≠ 0) turns into an arithmetic constant, "the cap on cancellation".

9

The height equation (gap ⑤): H(x([2]P)) = max{ |F(p,q)|, |G(p,q)| } / δ ≥ max{ |F(p,q)|, |G(p,q)| } / |4Δ′|.

▸ Reason F/δ and G/δ are integers with gcd = 1 (since we divided out δ), and G/δ = 4q⁴y²/δ > 0 (Step 4). So (F/δ)/(G/δ) is itself the reduced representation with positive denominator, and by uniqueness of the reduced representation (N1, V2) H equals its max. The second inequality is Step 8 (δ ≤ |4Δ′|). Where N2 got away with "≤", here we need an equality and an upper bound on δ ── because it's a lower bound.

Section 06

The lower bound and an explicit form of C₂ (gap ⑥)

10

Let κ := the maximum of "the sum of absolute values of the coefficients" of f₁, g₁, f₂, g₂. Then, as a bound for any homogeneous cubic,

|f₁(p,q)|, |g₁(p,q)|, |f₂(p,q)|, |g₂(p,q)| ≤ κ·T³ (T = max{|p|, |q|})

▸ Reason Each monomial of a homogeneous cubic satisfies |p|i|q|3−i ≤ T³ (triangle inequality). κ is a constant depending only on A, B.

11

Applying the triangle inequality to the two equations of Step 6:

|4Δ′|·T⁷ = max{ |4Δ′p⁷|, |4Δ′q⁷| } ≤ 2κT³ · max{ |F(p,q)|, |G(p,q)| }

▸ Reason |f₁F − g₁G| ≤ |f₁||F| + |g₁||G| ≤ κT³|F| + κT³|G| ≤ 2κT³ max{|F|, |G|}, and likewise on the p⁷ side. max{|p⁷|, |q⁷|} = T⁷ is the definition of max.

12

Dividing by T³ ≥ 1 and rearranging gives max{ |F|, |G| } ≥ (|4Δ′| / 2κ)·T⁴; together with Step 9:

H(x([2]P)) ≥ T⁴ / (2κ) = H(x(P))⁴ / (2κ)

▸ Reason Dividing by a positive number doesn't change the inequality (T ≥ 1, κ ≥ 1). The denominator |4Δ′| from Step 9 cancels here ── all that remains is the ratio of "how much cancellation can lose (≤ |4Δ′|)" to "how much the identities guarantee (≥ |4Δ′|T⁴/2κ)".

13

Finish by taking logarithms:

hx([2]P) ≥ 4hx(P) − log(2κ)
C₂(A, B) := max{ log 2κ, 4·max{ hx(P) : P ∈ E(ℚ)[2] } }

▸ Reason Monotonicity of log. The final max merges in the exceptional case of Step 1 (gaps ②⑥). This completes the proof of the lemma.

∎
Four times from above too: |h([2]P) − 4h(P)| ≤ C

Applying the same kind of "upper" bound as in N2 (ignoring cancellation, triangle inequality) to the same F, G also gives h([2]P) ≤ 4h(P) + log(κ′) (κ′ = the maximum of the sums of absolute values of the coefficients of F, G). Together, |h([2]P) − 4h(P)| ≤ C: this is what assumption (a) in Exercise 6 of the main part (Tate's telescoping trick) really is, leading directly to the existence of the canonical height ĥ = lim 4⁻ⁿh([2ⁿ]P)AEC VIII.9.

Section 07

Numerical check ─ E : y² = x³ + 3, 2P = (−23/16, −11/64) (gap ⑦)

With A = 0, B = 3, Δ′ = 27·9 = 243, 4Δ′ = 972, and

f₁ = 12X²Z, g₁ = 3X³ − 81Z³, f₂ = 972X³ + 2592Z³, g₂ = −5184XZ²
f₂ = 4Δ′X³ + 12B·8B²·Z³ = 972X³ + 12·3·72·Z³, g₂ = −2B·96B²·XZ² = −2·3·864·XZ² (only the terms that survive with A = 0)

For the coordinates (p, q) = (−23, 16) of 2P, with F(−23,16) = 2540833 and G(−23,16) = 7744:

CheckLeft sideRight sideResult
First identityf₁F − g₁G972·16⁷ = 260 919 263 232✓ match
Second identityf₂F − g₂G972·(−23)⁷ = −3 309 490 334 484✓ match
Cancellation δδ | gcd(972, 7744) = 4F is odd ⇒ δ = 1✓ consistent with the table in the main part
Lower boundH(x(4P)) = 2 540 833≥ T⁴/2κ = 23⁴/10368 ≈ 27✓ (holds with room to spare)

For this curve, the sums of absolute values of coefficients are f₁ : 12, g₁ : 84, f₂ : 3564, g₂ : 5184, so κ = 5184. E(ℚ)[2] = {O} (x³ = −3 has no rational root), so the torsion term disappears and

C₂ = log(2·5184) = log 10368 ≈ 9.25
so on this curve h([2]P) ≥ 4h(P) − 9.25 holds for every point. The measured h(4P) ≈ 4.7·h(2P) (from the table in the main part) sits comfortably inside this band

The bound is crude (the guarantee is 1/10368, versus the actual ratio 2540833/23⁴ ≈ 9.1), but what matters is that it's uniform, independent of P. Uniformity was all the descent theorem (N0) asked for.
