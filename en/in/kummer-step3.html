The inf–res exact sequence and unramifiedness
Inflation–Restriction and Unramified Classes

We completely prove the inflation–restriction exact sequence the main part passed over as "a formal cocycle computation" (this is also the complete solution to Exercise 4 of the main part). On top of that we fill in the commutativity of the commutative diagram for the reduction lemma (§05), that "unramified" doesn't depend on the choice of extension (the conjugation lemma), and a zero-leap reconstruction of Proposition VIII.2.1, completing the kummer between-the-lines notes. The last section is the starting point of the bridge to the "local conditions" of Selmer groups (Main V).

Section 02

Fixing the premises (J1–J4)

Premise J1 ─ setting up subgroups and quotients

Let G be a profinite group and M a discrete G-module (N0 §04). In the inf–res theorem, N ⊴ G is an open normal subgroup (every application in this series is of this kind. A fact verified in N0 Step 9: MN becomes a G/N-module via mσN := mσ ── normality of N is what makes changing representatives and well-definedness work). res is an operation that doesn't need normality, so we define it for any closed subgroup H ≤ G (the inertia group Iv̄ is this case). In applications N = GK/L (L/K finite Galois), and G/N ≅ GL/Kweakmw N1 premise Q4 / ANT IV.1.2.

Premise J2 ─ decomposition groups, inertia groups and conjugacy of extensions weakmw N2 premise R2 / ANT I §9, II §9

Let v be a finite place of K and v̄ an extension of it to K. The decomposition group Dv̄/v = { σ : v̄∘σ = v̄ } and the inertia group Iv̄/v ⊂ Dv̄/v (those inducing the identity on the residue field) are closed subgroups of G. G acts transitively on the extensions of vANT II.8.1 (in the limit to K), and when v̄′ = v̄∘σ, directly from the definitions,

Iv̄′/v = σ−1 Iv̄/v σ (likewise for decomposition groups)

That is, changing the extension conjugates the inertia group.

Premise J3 ─ parts from weakmw N2 (reduction and inertia) weakmw N2 §04–§05 / AEC VIII.1.5(b)

Let S := {places of bad reduction} ∪ { v : v(m) ≠ 0 } ∪ MK∞. weakmw N2 proved the following with zero leaps: if v ∉ S, v̄ is an extension, σ ∈ Iv̄/v, and [m]Q = P ∈ E(K), then Qσ = Q (§05 Steps 3–9. The tools are the reduction map via primitive coordinates and its compatibility with Galois (§04), and that E[m] maps injectively under reduction when there is good reduction and v ∤ m). Moreover this argument doesn't use E[m] ⊂ E(K).

Premise J4 ─ the machines of N0 and N1

Rules for computing with cocycles (N0 §03: in particular ce = 0 = Step 3), descent of continuous cocycles (N0 §04), the six-term sequence (N0 §07), the Kummer exact sequence for elliptic curves and δ = κ (N1 §07–§08). Notation as in N0 R1–R3 (right action, additive).

Section 03

Constructing inf and res (gap ①)

1

Constructing res: for a closed subgroup H ≤ G, the restriction res : H¹(G, M) → H¹(H, M), [c] ↦ [c|H] is a well-defined group homomorphism.

▸ Reason M is a discrete H-module by restricting the action (stabilizers are open in H too: intersections with open sets). c|H is a cocycle: the condition cστ = cστ + cτ holds in particular for pairs σ, τ ∈ H. Continuity: the restriction of a continuous map is continuous. The restriction of a coboundary is a coboundary (the same m works), so it's well defined as a map on classes. Homomorphism: restriction commutes with pointwise sums. Note that normality is used nowhere (a check needed for using it with inertia groups in §06).

2

Constructing inf: for an open normal subgroup N ⊴ G, the pullback inf : H¹(G/N, MN) → H¹(G, M), (inf c̄)σ := c̄σN is a well-defined group homomorphism.

▸ Reason That MN is a G/N-module was checked in N0 Step 9 (normality). Cocycle condition: (inf c̄)στ = c̄(σN)(τN) = (c̄σN)τN + c̄τN = ((inf c̄)σ)τ + (inf c̄)τ (just unwinding the definition mσN = mσ of the G/N action on MN). Continuity: inf c̄ is constant on each coset σN (an open set), i.e. locally constant. It preserves coboundaries: the pullback of c̄σN = mσN − m (m ∈ MN) is σ ↦ mσ − m, the G-coboundary of the same m. Homomorphism: pointwise.

3

res ∘ inf = 0 (the composition vanishes ── half of the "⊇ side" of the exact sequence).

▸ Reason For η ∈ N, (inf c̄)η = c̄ηN = c̄eN = 0. The last equality is "cocycles vanish at the identity" (N0 Step 3 applied to the quotient group G/N). That is, the restriction is not just a coboundary but the zero map itself.

Section 04 ─ Main

The inf–res exact sequence (gap ②)

Theorem (inflation–restriction) AEC B.2.4

If G is a profinite group, N ⊴ G an open normal subgroup and M a discrete G-module, the following is exact:

0 → H¹(G/N, MN) →inf H¹(G, M) →res H¹(N, M)

(In Galois language with N = GK/L: 0 → H¹(GL/K, MGK/L) → H¹(GK/K, M) → H¹(GK/L, M).)

4

Injectivity of inf (exact at the left end).

▸ Reason Suppose inf c̄ is a coboundary of G, i.e. c̄σN = mσ − m (∀σ, for some m ∈ M). Plugging in η ∈ N, the left side is c̄eN = 0 (Step 3), so mη = m (∀η ∈ N), i.e. m ∈ MN. Then σN ↦ mσN − m is the G/N-coboundary defined by the element m of MN, and it equals c̄. So [c̄] = 0.

5

ker res ⊆ im inf (exact in the middle; the other direction is Step 3).

▸ Reason Suppose res[c] = 0: for some m ∈ M, cη = mη − m (∀η ∈ N). Setting c′ := c − (the coboundary of m), [c′] = [c] and c′|N = 0. We now examine c′. (a) Constant on left cosets: for η ∈ N, σ ∈ G, using the cocycle condition in the order (η, σ), c′ησ = (c′η)σ + c′σ = c′σ. (b) Values lie in MN: using it in the order (σ, η), c′ση = (c′σ)η + c′η = (c′σ)η. On the other hand, by normality of N, ση ∈ σN = Nσ, so by (a) c′ση = c′σ. Equating, (c′σ)η = c′σ (∀η ∈ N). (c) By (a)(b), c̄σN := c′σ is a well-defined map G/N → MN, and the cocycle condition descends from that of c′ (the same check as N0 Step 9). Continuity: c′ is continuous (a difference of a continuous cocycle and a continuous coboundary), and G/N is finite discrete (N is open), so c̄ is automatically continuous. (d) By definition inf c̄ = c′. So [c] = [c′] = inf[c̄]. ∎

6

Returning the favor to N0 §04: this proves that "the union H¹cont(G, M) = ⋃U inf(H¹(G/U, MU)) increases without overlap".

▸ Reason The homework announced in N0 §04's why box, "that inf is injective at the level of classes is a consequence of N3". The injectivity in Step 4 is what it contains. The compatibility of the two-stage inf for U ⊆ U′ (transitivity of inf) is just lining up the composed definitions c̄σU = c̄̄σU′, and that the values don't change when passing through an intermediate quotient also follows from the definitions.

? What does this exact sequence compare with what?

inf–res is a bridge between "information at the finite stage G/N" and "information at the infinite stage G". Injectivity at the left end says "classes that differ at the finite stage differ at the infinite stage too", and exactness in the middle says "if a class at the infinite stage vanishes on N, it actually comes from the finite stage". The reason Part 2 (hand computations in the finite group GL/K) and Part 3 (general theory with the absolute Galois group) can go back and forth is condensed into these two claims.

Section 05

What the reduction lemma really is ─ checking the commutative diagram (gap ③)

Fix the setting of §05 in the main part: L/K a finite Galois extension, E[m] ⊂ E(L), N := GK/L (open normal), G/N ≅ GL/K. For M = E[m], MN = E[m] ∩ E(L) = E[m], so inf–res becomes

0 → H¹(GL/K, E[m]) →inf H¹(GK/K, E[m]) →res H¹(GK/L, E[m])

(the bottom row of the diagram in §05 of the main part). The top row is 0 → Φ → E(K)/mE(K) → E(L)/mE(L), with kernel Φ := ( E(K) ∩ mE(L) )/mE(K). What we need to check is the commutativity of the two squares.

7

The right square: res ∘ δK = δL ∘ (the natural map E(K)/mE(K) → E(L)/mE(L)).

▸ Reason Take P ∈ E(K) and choose one Q with [m]Q = P. A representative of δK(P) is σ ↦ Qσ − Q (σ ∈ GK/K), and its restriction to GK/L is the same formula read for σ ∈ GK/L. On the other hand, δL(P), viewing P as an element of E(L), is also the same formula if we choose the same Q as the lift (the freedom of choice in N0 Step 13). The cocycles agree literally, so the classes agree.

8

Constructing the left vertical arrow: if P ∈ E(K) ∩ mE(L), then δK(P) ∈ ker res = im inf, and the explicit form of its preimage under inf is exactly Part 2's λP:

λP ∈ Z¹(GL/K, E[m]), λP(σ|L) = Qσ − Q (Q ∈ E(L), [m]Q = P)

▸ Reason Since P ∈ mE(L), we can choose the lift Q inside E(L). Then for σ ∈ GK/L, Qσ = Q, so res δK(P) = 0, and by exactness (Step 5) δK(P) ∈ im inf. Moreover this representative cocycle has values Qσ − Q in E[m] (applying [m] gives Pσ − P = O), and since Q's coordinates are elements of L, the action of σ is determined by its restriction σ|L alone (L/K is Galois, so Qσ ∈ E(L)). That is, it's well defined as a map on GL/K, and it agrees with the result of applying the construction of Step 5 (reading c′ coset by coset) to the present c. It's the same formula, symbol for symbol, as the λP handmade in weakmw N0weakmw N0 §04 / AEC VIII.1.1.1.

9

Checking the three-line proof of the reduction lemma: λ : Φ → H¹(GL/K, E[m]), P mod mE(K) ↦ [λP] is a well-defined injection. The right side is a finite set. So Φ is finite.

▸ Reason Well-definedness and injectivity by diagram chasing: inf[λP] = δK(P) (Step 8), δK is injective on E(K)/mE(K) (N1 Step 10), and inf is injective too (Step 4). So [λP] = [λP′] ⟺ δK(P) = δK(P′) ⟺ P ≡ P′ (mod mE(K)). Finiteness: GL/K and E[m] are both finite sets, so cocycles are among the maps from a finite set to a finite set, Z¹ ⊆ Map(GL/K, E[m]) is finite, and so is its quotient H¹. The finiteness of the kernel Φ, used in Part 2's reduction lemma (VIII.1.1.1) as "E(L)/mE(L) finite ⇒ E(K)/mE(K) finite", has been reproved in the language of cohomology.

? What were Part 2's "hand computations" doing?

The proof of injectivity of λP in STEP 0 of Part 2 was carrying out "commutativity of the diagram + injectivity of inf + injectivity of δ", which we just saw, all at once, with bare hands. The benefit of the general theory is division of labor: injectivity splits into three parts, and each part (Step 4, N1 Step 10, Steps 7–8) can be reused in other situations. "Unramified" from §06 on is exactly where that reuse happens.

Section 06

The conjugation lemma ─ "unramified" doesn't depend on the choice of extension (gap ④)

Definition (unramified cohomology classes, restated) AEC VIII §2

Let v be a finite place of K, v̄ an extension, and Iv̄/v ⊂ G its inertia group (J2; it's a closed subgroup, so the res of Step 1 can be used). ξ ∈ H¹(G, M) is unramified at v if resIv̄/v(ξ) = 0 ∈ H¹(Iv̄/v, M).

It would be a problem if this definition depended on v̄. By J2 the inertia group of another extension is the conjugate σ−1 Iv̄/v σ, so the following lemma is all there is to "it doesn't depend".

Lemma (the conjugation lemma) the content of AEC X.4.1.1 / Exercise B.6

Let c ∈ Z¹(G, M), H ≤ G a closed subgroup, σ ∈ G. If c|H is a coboundary of H, then c|σ⁻¹Hσ is a coboundary of σ−1Hσ too. Moreover the "witness" can be written explicitly: if cη = mη − m (∀η ∈ H), then

with n := mσ − cσ, cτ = nτ − n (∀τ ∈ σ−1Hσ)
10

Proof: expand στ = ησ in two ways.

▸ Reason Take τ ∈ σ−1Hσ and set η := στσ−1 ∈ H, so στ = ησ. Write cστ in two ways with the cocycle condition: (1) cστ = (cσ)τ + cτ. (2) cησ = (cη)σ + cσ = (mη − m)σ + cσ = mησ − mσ + cσ (the assumption and R1(i)(ii)). Equating and rearranging, cτ = mησ − mσ + cσ − (cσ)τ. On the other hand, for n := mσ − cσ, nτ − n = mστ − (cσ)τ − mσ + cσ. Since στ = ησ, mστ = mησ, and the two expressions agree. ∎ (One can also go through the inverse formula cσ⁻¹ = −(cσ)σ⁻¹ of N0 Step 4, but the two-way expansion above is shorter.)

Corollary (well-definedness of unramifiedness)

For ξ = [c] ∈ H¹(G, M) and two extensions v̄, v̄′ of v, resIv̄/v(ξ) = 0 ⟺ resIv̄′/v(ξ) = 0. Reason: by J2 there is σ with Iv̄′/v = σ−1 Iv̄/v σ, and the condition "the restriction is a coboundary" is transported in both directions by the conjugation lemma (⟸ is the same lemma with σ replaced by σ−1). So "ξ is unramified at v" is a notion independent of the extension, and the parenthetical in the definition box of §06 in the main part (AEC X.4.1.1 / Exercise B.6) is now verified.

Section 07

Reconstructing Proposition VIII.2.1 (gap ⑤)

Proposition AEC VIII.2.1

Let S = {bad reduction} ∪ { v : v(m) ≠ 0 } ∪ MK∞. Every class in the image of δ : E(K)/mE(K) → H¹(G, E[m]) is unramified at every finite place v not in S.

11

Turning the claim into cocycles: what we must show is "for v ∉ S and σ ∈ Iv̄/v, cσ = Qσ − Q = O". If we can say this, the restriction is not just a coboundary but the zero cocycle.

▸ Reason A representative of δ(P) is cσ = Qσ − Q ([m]Q = P, N1 Step 13). By the corollary of §06, it's enough to choose one extension v̄. "0 as a cocycle" ⇒ "0 as a class" is the trivial direction. Note that the definition (§06) only requires vanishing up to a coboundary, but here the strong form (identically O) holds.

12

Connecting the parts from weakmw N2: if v ∉ S and σ ∈ Iv̄/v, then Qσ = Q. So the proposition follows.

▸ Reason This is exactly the content of the main theorem of weakmw N2 §05 Steps 3–9 cited in J3: Qσ − Q ∈ E[m] (applying [m] gives Pσ − P = O); by Galois compatibility of the reduction map (weakmw N2 §04) and "elements of inertia induce the identity on the residue field", the reduction red(Qσ − Q) = Õ; and for v ∉ S (good reduction and v ∤ m) E[m] maps injectively under reduction, so Qσ − Q = O. Every link in this chain has been verified with zero leaps in weakmw N2, and the assumption E[m] ⊂ E(K) is used nowhere (confirming the parenthetical in the proof in §06 of the main part). ∎

With this, the "compressed form" in §06 of the main part is verified, with all its parts:

E(K)/mE(K) ↪ H¹(GK/K, E[m]; S) := { ξ : unramified at every finite place outside S }
Injectivity is N1 Step 10; that the image lands in the right side is this section; and that the right side is a subgroup is clear as an intersection of kernels, since res is a homomorphism (Step 1).
What this note does not prove (scope declaration)

The finiteness of the right side H¹(G, E[m]; S) is not proved here. As the main part says, "what's inside is the argument of STEP 3 in Part 2 itself", and formally it is cited and proved as AEC X.4.3 in Part 5 (Main V, Selmer and Ш)AEC X.4.3. On the weakmw side, its substance was weakmw N3 (the Kummer correspondence, finiteness of the class number, S-units). In other words, the proof of finiteness already exists elsewhere in this series ── there's no new gap to add here.

Section 08

Laying the groundwork for Selmer groups ─ the language of local conditions (gap ⑥)

Looking back, all the tools built in this note serve a single pattern: "restrict a global class to a local subgroup and impose a condition":

ToolRoleProof in this note
res : H¹(G, M) → H¹(H, M)A device to "impose" local conditions (H = Iv̄, Dv̄, GK/L…)Step 1
Conjugation lemmaGuarantees the condition depends only on "the place v" (not on the choice of extension)Step 10
inf–res exact sequenceA device that pins down the "origin" of classes satisfying the condition at a finite stageStep 4–5

Selmer groups (Main V) are a refinement of this pattern. "Unramified" was a condition imposed by restricting to the inertia group Iv, while Selmer groups impose it by restricting to the whole decomposition group Gv:

S(m)(E/K) = { ξ ∈ H¹(G, E[m]) : for all v, resGv(ξ) comes from local points E(Kv)/mE(Kv) }
"unramified" = vanishes on inertia ⊂ "locally solvable" = lies in the image of δ at the level of decomposition groups (precise definitions and comparison in Main V §03–§04)

The relation between the two conditions (for almost all v, "locally solvable ⇒ unramified", and with corrections at finitely many v the Selmer group fits into the finite box H¹(G, E[m]; S)) is the skeleton of the finiteness proof in Main V (AEC X.4.2–X.4.3). The homomorphism property of res, conjugation well-definedness and inf–res are used there without comment ── and this note has justified that lack of comment.

▸ The starting point of the bridge to the selmer series

Main V (selmer.html) doesn't have between-the-lines notes yet. The first candidate when making them is "comparing local conditions (unramified vs locally solvable) and the finiteness of X.4.2–X.4.3", and its prerequisites are all in place in Steps 1, 4–5 and 10 of this page and in weakmw N3 (the substance of finiteness).
