Between-the-lines notes ─ The Kummer pairing N1/N3

The Kummer exact sequence for elliptic curves
Plugging E into the Six-Term Machine

Into the six-term machine assembled in N0, we plug A = E[m], B = C = E(K), ψ = [m]. For the Kummer exact sequence the main part described in one line as "cut it out and you get it", we fill in, without skipping a line: a check that E(K) meets the machine's operating conditions (a discrete G-module), consistency of the two meanings of the symbol [m], the cutting-out procedure itself, and finally that δ literally coincides with the Kummer pairing κ of Part 2.

TEXT: Silverman, AEC 2nd ed. ─ Ch. VIII §2 (fully expanded)

Main III The Kummer pairing N0 Foundations of cohomology N1 The Kummer sequence N2 Hilbert 90 N3 inf–res and unramified
Section 01

The gaps this note fills

  • ① That E(K) and E[m] fit N0 §04's framework of "discrete G-modules" ── the first half of the homework N0 announced with "verified in N1 and N2"
  • ② That 0 → E[m] → E(K) → E(K) → 0 is a short exact sequence of G-modules (exactness at 3 places and G-equivariance of [m])
  • ③ That the left three terms of the six-term sequence turn into E(K)[m] → E(K) → E(K) ── checking E(K)G = E(K)
  • ④ That the right-end map [m]* agrees with "the multiplication-by-m map of the group H¹". The main part's notation H¹(G, E)[m] uses this agreement implicitly
  • ⑤ The cutting-out procedure: checking each stage of making the short exact sequence 0 → E(K)/mE(K) → H¹(G, E[m]) → H¹(G, E)[m] → 0 from the six-term sequence
  • ⑥ Checking δ = κ, and the mechanism by which "the difference by an element of mE(K)" vanishes from the start, without even needing to drop to a coboundary

We fix the premises with the D series in §02. The R series of N0 and its results (the six-term sequence, the continuous cocycle theorem, f*, the construction of δ) carry over as is. After reading this note, every line of §03 of the main part can be traced all the way to its root.

Section 02

Fixing the premises (D1–D4)

Premise D1 ─ the stage (field and Galois group)

Let K be a field of characteristic 0 (a number field in mind), K a fixed algebraic closure, and G := GK/K. KG = K (char 0, so K/K is Galois. weakmw N1 premise Q2). G is a profinite group with the Krull topology, and for a finite extension L/K, GK/L is an open subgroup ANT IV.1.2.

Premise D2 ─ group structure of the point set and the Galois action AEC III.2.2 / III.3.6

Let E/K be an elliptic curve given by a Weierstrass equation with coefficients in K. E(K) is an abelian group, and addition and inverses are given by morphisms with coefficients in K. σ ∈ G acts by P = (x, y) ↦ Pσ = (xσ, yσ), Oσ = O, and commutes with addition: (P + Q)σ = Pσ + Qσ (because the chord-and-tangent formulas are rational functions with coefficients in KAEC III.2.3; fully expanded in weakmw N0 premise P2). Composition is read with the right-action convention: Pστ = (Pσ)τweakmw N1 premise Q3. This is the substance of (i)(ii) among N0's R1(i)(ii)(iii); (iii) (the identity moves nothing) is trivial coordinate by coordinate.

Premise D3 ─ [m] and E[m] AEC III.6.2 / III.6.4

Let m ≥ 2. [m] : E → E is an isogeny of degree m²AEC III.6.2(a), and since it's m-fold repeated addition it commutes with the G-action: ([m]P)σ = [m](Pσ) (iterating the commutativity in D2). E[m] := ker [m] ⊂ E(K). In char 0, E[m] ≅ (ℤ/mℤ)², in particular #E[m] = m²AEC III.6.4. [m] : E(K) → E(K) is surjective (a nonconstant morphism is surjective on points over an algebraically closed fieldAEC II.2.3; full proof in weakmw N1 premise Q1).

Premise D4 ─ the machine of N0 (parts used as is)

We cite the following parts assembled in N0: the six-term sequence and exactness at 5 places (N0 §07), descending continuous cocycles to a finite stage (N0 §04), the construction of f* (N0 Step 10), and the construction and well-definedness of the connecting homomorphism δ (N0 Steps 11–14). E(K) is an additive group, so N0's additive notation applies as is.

Section 03

E(K) and E[m] are discrete G-modules (gap ①)

The machine of N0 §04 came with the operating condition "profinite group G + discrete G-module". That E(K) satisfies this condition is never checked in the main part. We fill that in here.

1

E(K) is a G-module (satisfies N0's R1 (i)(ii)(iii)).

▸ Reason First, the action "sends points to points": if P = (x, y) ∈ E(K) satisfies the Weierstrass equation, the coefficients of the equation are elements of K fixed by σ, so applying σ to both sides shows (xσ, yσ) satisfies the same equation. (i) Each σ is a group homomorphism: (P+Q)σ = Pσ + Qσ from D2. (ii) The composition convention Pστ = (Pσ)τ is composing the field action on coordinates (weakmw N1 premise Q3). (iii) Pe = P is the definition of the identity map.

2

The stabilizer of each point contains an open subgroup. Hence E(K) is a discrete G-module (fits the framework of N0 §04). Equivalently:

E(K) = ⋃L E(L) (L running over finite extensions of K)

▸ Reason The coordinates x, y of P = (x, y) ∈ E(K) are algebraic over K, so LP := K(x, y) is a finite extension. σ ∈ GK/LP fixes x and y, so Pσ = P. That is, the stabilizer of P contains the open subgroup GK/LP (D1). The reverse inclusion ⋃ E(L) ⊆ E(K) is trivial, and ⊇ is the construction of LP just given. O is invariant under every σ (D2).

3

E[m] is a finite G-submodule of E(K) (and hence also a discrete G-module).

▸ Reason It's a subgroup because it's the kernel of the homomorphism [m] (D3). G-stability: if T ∈ E[m], then [m](Tσ) = ([m]T)σ = Oσ = O (commutativity in D3), i.e. Tσ ∈ E[m]. Finiteness is #E[m] = m² (D3). What the weak MW main part said, "the coordinates of each point of E[m] are algebraic numbers in K"AEC III.6.4, is recovered as a special case of the argument in Step 2.

? What "factoring through a finite extension" really looks like

The equation E(K) = ⋃ E(L) of Step 2 is where it meshes with the theorem of N0 §04. A continuous cocycle with values in E[m] can be written, once you take one finite Galois extension L ⊇ K(E[m]), as the inflation of a cocycle of GL/K. Part 2 could get by throughout with "hand computations in the finite group GL/K" thanks to these two facts combined.

Section 04

Checking the short exact sequence (gap ②)

Lemma (the Kummer short exact sequence) AEC VIII §2

The following is a short exact sequence of discrete G-modules and G-equivariant homomorphisms:

0 → E[m] →ι E(K) →[m] E(K) → 0
4

ι (inclusion) is an injective G-equivariant homomorphism.

▸ Reason An inclusion map is an injective homomorphism. G-equivariance is exactly that E[m] is a G-submodule (Step 3) ── including a submodule is equivariant by definition.

5

im ι = ker [m]. That is, exact in the middle.

▸ Reason E[m] := ker [m] (definition in D3), so the image of the inclusion is exactly the kernel. All there is to check is matching definitions, but confirming that N0 R2's promise "identify A ⊆ B, and the kernel of ψ is exactly A" is literally satisfied here is the first half of gap ②.

6

[m] : E(K) → E(K) is a surjective G-equivariant homomorphism. This establishes the short exact sequence.

▸ Reason Homomorphism: [m] is repeated addition (D3). Surjective: AEC II.2.3 + III.6.2(a) (D3; full proof in weakmw N1 premise Q1). G-equivariance: ([m]P)σ = [m](Pσ) (D3). A note on continuity: it's a map between discrete modules, so continuity of the map itself isn't an issue (same as the note in N0 Step 10).

Section 05

Running the six-term sequence (gap ③)

Steps 1–6 confirmed all the operating conditions, so we can apply the theorem of N0 §07 to A = E[m], B = C = E(K), ψ = [m]:

0 → E[m]G → E(K)G →[m] E(K)G →δ H¹(G, E[m]) →ι* H¹(G, E(K)) →[m]* H¹(G, E(K))

All that's left is to compute the invariant parts.

7

E(K)G = E(K), and E[m]G = E(K)[m].

▸ Reason (⊇) If P ∈ E(K), its coordinates are in K, so it's fixed by every σ. O is fixed too (D2). (⊆) If P = (x, y) is fixed by every σ, then x, y ∈ KG = K (D1), i.e. P ∈ E(K). Second half: E[m]G = E[m] ∩ E(K)G = E[m] ∩ E(K) = { P ∈ E(K) : [m]P = O } = E(K)[m].

8

Hence the six-term sequence agrees with the second formula of §03 in the main part:

0 → E(K)[m] → E(K) →[m] E(K) →δ H¹(G, E[m]) →ι* H¹(G, E(K)) →[m]* H¹(G, E(K))

▸ Reason Just substituting the equations of Step 7. That the maps between the left three terms are "inclusion" and "restriction of [m]" comes from "checking where things land" in N0 Step 15 (φ(AG) ⊆ BG, etc.). From now on, following the main part's shorthand, we write H¹(G, E) := H¹(G, E(K)).

Section 06

[m]* is "multiplication by m on H¹" (gap ④)

Lemma (the induced map agrees with multiplication by m)

On H¹(G, E(K)), the map induced by the isogeny [m] agrees with multiplying classes by m:

[m]* [c] = m·[c] (∀[c] ∈ H¹(G, E(K)))
9

Proof.

▸ Reason The definition of [m]* (N0 Step 10) is [c] ↦ [[m]∘c]. For each σ, ([m]∘c)σ = [m](cσ) = cσ + ⋯ + cσ (a sum of m terms, definition of [m]). On the other hand, the group structure on Z¹ is the pointwise sum (N0 Step 1), so the m-fold element mc of the cocycle c also has (mc)σ = cσ + ⋯ + cσ. So [m]∘c = mc are identical as cocycles. Taking classes, [m]*[c] = [mc] = m[c] (m times in the quotient group).

? Why this counts as a "gap"

The symbol [m] is "an isogeny of E", while multiplication by m is "repeating the operation of the abelian group H¹" ── they live in different worlds. The moment the main part wrote H¹(G, E)[m] (the m-torsion subgroup), the agreement of these two was being used implicitly. Checking it takes the one step above, but without checking it we have no right to identify "the kernel of [m]*" with "m-torsion".

Section 07 ─ Main

Cutting out the Kummer exact sequence (gap ⑤)

What's inside the main part's "cutting out the middle" is the following three checks. We use the exactness of the six-term sequence (N0 Steps 17–19) as parts.

10

δ induces a well-defined injective homomorphism δ̄ : E(K)/mE(K) ↪ H¹(G, E[m]).

▸ Reason δ : E(K) → H¹(G, E[m]) is a group homomorphism (N0 Step 14). Its kernel, by exactness at CG (N0 Step 17), is ker δ = [m](E(K)) = mE(K). By the first isomorphism theorem (the same standard fact as weakmw N0 premise P5), δ induces an injective homomorphism δ̄ : E(K)/mE(K) ↪ H¹(G, E[m]), with im δ̄ = im δ.

11

im ι* = H¹(G, E)[m]. That is, ι* maps onto the m-torsion subgroup of H¹(G, E).

▸ Reason By exactness at H¹(G, B) (N0 Step 19), im ι* = ker [m]*. By Step 9, ker [m]* = { [c] : m[c] = 0 } = H¹(G, E)[m]. Just connect the two.

12

Putting it together, the following sequence is exact (all 3 places checked):

0 → E(K)/mE(K) →δ̄ H¹(G, E[m]) →ι* H¹(G, E)[m] → 0

▸ Reason Left end (injectivity of δ̄): Step 10. Middle (ker ι* = im δ̄): exactness at H¹(G, A) (N0 Step 18) gives ker ι* = im δ, and substitute im δ = im δ̄ from Step 10. Right end (surjectivity of ι*): Step 11 showed the image is exactly H¹(G, E)[m] ── since we replaced "the target group" by this image, surjectivity holds by definition.

Theorem (the Kummer exact sequence for elliptic curves) AEC VIII §2
0 → E(K)/mE(K) →δ H¹(G, E[m]) → H¹(G, E)[m] → 0

With this, every line of the box stating the claim in §03 of the main part has been verified.

Section 08

δ = κ ─ how the difference by mE(K) vanishes (gap ⑥)

13

The cocycle representation of δ is exactly the Kummer pairing of Part 2: for P ∈ E(K), choosing Q with [m]Q = P,

δ(P) = [ σ ↦ Qσ − Q ] = [ σ ↦ κ(P, σ) ]

▸ Reason In the construction of N0 Step 11, choosing Q as the lift b (it exists by the surjectivity of Step 6) gives dσ = Qσ − Q. This matches, symbol for symbol, the defining formula of κ in §01 of the main part and weakmw N1 §03AEC VIII §1. Because of "the freedom to choose the lift" (N0 Step 13), we may make this particular choice.

14

Two-stage invariance under replacement. (i) Under Q → Q + T (T ∈ E[m]) the cocycle changes only by a coboundary. (ii) Under P → P + [m]R (R ∈ E(K)), with a good choice of lift the cocycle is literally identical.

▸ Reason (i) (Q+T)σ − (Q+T) = (Qσ − Q) + (Tσ − T). The second term is the coboundary defined by T (a concrete instance of N0 Step 13 with a = −T). So the class doesn't change. (ii) Q + R can be taken as a lift of P + [m]R ([m](Q+R) = P + [m]R). Then (Q+R)σ − (Q+R) = (Qσ − Q) + (Rσ − R) = Qσ − Q. The last equality is because R ∈ E(K) = E(K)G (Step 7) gives Rσ = R. Not even a coboundary appears.

? "A two-layer cancellation" ── what the intuition really is

The freedom coming from E[m] (how to choose the answer to the division) is absorbed by the quotient by B¹ in cohomology. The difference by mE(K) (an m-fold shift of the point being divided) vanishes completely through the choice of lift, without even relying on the quotient. This two-layer structure is what's inside "δ̄ is a well-defined injection on E(K)/mE(K)", and it's the precise form of the main part's slogan, "only the twist in division remains as information".

The three items of Part 2's Proposition VIII.1.2, which §03 of the main part said "come out automatically from the general theory of cohomology", correspond to the following parts:

(a) well-definedness of κSteps 13–14 (a concrete instance of N0 Step 13)
(b) bilinearity (on the P side and σ side)P side: δ is a homomorphism (N0 Step 14). σ side: the cocycle condition (N0 Step 12)
(c) left kernel = mE(K)Step 10 (N0 Step 17)
Section 09

What the three terms classify (sorting out the intuition)

TermWhat it classifiesWhere it leads
E(K)/mE(K) "Remainders mod m" of rational points. The very object whose generators we want Input to descent (Main IV)
H¹(G, E[m]) The whole collection of types of "Galois twist when trying to divide P by m". If E[m] ⊂ E(K), it degenerates to Hom(G, E[m]) (N0 Step 5) = information about field extensions Part 2's L = K([m]−1E(K)) is a restatement in the language of this group
H¹(G, E)[m] The global obstruction to division. Later identified with the m-torsion of the Weil–Châtelet group (the group of classes of principal homogeneous spaces of E)AEC X §3 / X.3.6 The home of the stars of Part 5 (the Selmer group and Ш)

What the cut-out exact sequence says is: the group we want to know, E(K)/mE(K), embeds injectively into "the space of types of twist", and the remainder is measured exactly by "the group of obstructions". If you follow the same procedure for the multiplicative group K×, the right end vanishes and the theory closes (Hilbert 90), whereas for elliptic curves it doesn't vanish. This asymmetry is the starting point of all of Part 5 (proved in N2).

? Why the exact sequence alone doesn't give finiteness

H¹(G, E[m]) is a huge group (even when E[m] ⊂ E(K) it's Hom(G, (ℤ/m)²), and every cyclic extension of K of degree m contributes a cocycle). The target of the injection δ̄ is too big, so as it stands we can say nothing about the finiteness of E(K)/mE(K). Cutting down the image with "unramified conditions" and squeezing it into a finite box is the job of §06 in the main part ── its full expansion is planned for N3 (inflation–restriction and unramifiedness).
