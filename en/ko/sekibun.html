Seeing integrals as "area"

Trial III ── Riemann's wall soldiers block the way. Stack up infinitely thin rectangles and expose the true nature of area. Why does "the reverse of a derivative" find area in one go?

∫ab f(x) dx = the area under the curve

① The thinner the strips, the more accurate

Fill the space under the curve with "thin rectangles (strips)" to approximate the area. The more strips, the closer you get to the real area.

② With integrals, you get the area in one go!

The limit of infinitely thin strips = the integral ∫f(x)dx gives the area in one go!

Summary

① Fill the space under the curve with thin rectangles → more strips, more accuracy
The limit as they get infinitely thin is what the integral ∫f(x)dx means.
② Using the indefinite integral (antiderivative) F(x),
∫ab f(x)dx = F(b) − F(a) computes it in one go.
You don't need to count infinitely many strips: a single subtraction gives the answer.
