Ruins II ― The Sanctuary of Symmetry

Sealed skill "Group Axioms" ── associativity, identity, inverses. From the inscriptions of groups, subgroups, symmetric groups and Cayley tables, master a third eye for seeing structure.

0What is a group?
A group is a "set with an operation" that satisfies four axioms.
Addition of integers, multiplication of matrices, symmetries of shapes… all fit the same framework.
The 4 group axioms:
① Closure: a,b∈G → a★b∈G
② Associativity: (a★b)★c = a★(b★c)
③ Identity: there is an e with e★a = a★e = a
④ Inverses: there is an a⁻¹ with a★a⁻¹ = a⁻¹★a = e
1The symmetry group of the equilateral triangle D₃
All the moves that "put an equilateral triangle back in the same shape" form a group.
Press the buttons and try each move.
2Cayley table (operation table)
A Cayley table lists the group operation in a table.
Every element appears exactly once in each row and each column (a Latin square).
★err²s₁s₂s₃
e err²s₁s₂s₃
r rr²es₃s₁s₂
r²r²ers₂s₃s₁
s₁s₁s₂s₃err²
s₂s₂s₃s₁r²er
s₃s₃s₁s₂rr²e
The diagonal (gold) shows each element applied twice.
s★s=e (a reflection undoes itself), r★r=r² (rotations add up).
r★s₁ ≠ s₁★r (non-commutative!) ← D₃ is not an abelian group.
3Subgroups and Lagrange's theorem
If a subset H of a group G satisfies the group axioms, H is called a subgroup.
Lagrange's theorem: |H| divides |G|.
The order of D₃ is 6. Subgroups can only have order 1, 2, 3, 6.
{e} (order 1), {e,s₁} (order 2), {e,r,r²} (order 3, normal subgroup), D₃ itself (order 6)
There can be no subgroup of order 4 or 5! (They don't divide 6.)

The heart of group theory — 4 keywords

Group (G, ★)
A set with an operation satisfying the 4 axioms (closure, associativity, identity, inverses).
Cayley table
A table of all results of the operation. Every element appears once in each row and column.
Subgroup H ≤ G
A subset of G that is itself a group.
Lagrange's theorem
|H| ∣ |G|. The order of a subgroup must divide the order of the whole group.
