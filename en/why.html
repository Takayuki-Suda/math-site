Plimpton 322 ── The Oldest Mathematical Record in the World

In the early 1900s, a single clay tablet was found in what is now southern Iraq. A small tablet, 9 cm tall and 13 cm wide. It would go on to rewrite the history of mathematics.

🔗 See a photo of the real tablet (Wikipedia)

This tablet records the side lengths of right triangles across 15 rows. For example, the first row is "119, 120, 169" —— and indeed 119² + 120² = 169². This was more than 1,000 years before Pythagoras.

Timeline

3,800 Years of Mathematics

🏛️
1800 BCE ── Babylonia
Plimpton 322
A table of right triangles, thought to have been used for building temples, canals, and ziggurats. The oldest known record of trigonometry.
🏺
c. 570 BCE ── Greece
The Pythagorean Theorem
Pythagoras is the one who "proved" a² + b² = c². The Babylonians were already using it, but the Greeks were the ones who showed why it is true.
🍎
1600s ── Europe
Newton and Leibniz invent calculus
Born to calculate the motion of planets, the design of bridges, and falling objects. The foundation of modern physics and engineering.
🤖
Today ── The 21st Century
AI, Space, Medicine, Cryptography
Training ChatGPT, computing rocket trajectories, processing MRI images —— all of it rests on linear algebra, calculus, and probability.
"The mathematical world is only just waking up to the fact that this ancient but very sophisticated mathematical culture has much to teach us."
— Dr. Daniel Mansfield, UNSW (Plimpton 322 researcher)
