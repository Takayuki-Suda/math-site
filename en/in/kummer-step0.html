Between-the-lines notes ─ The Kummer pairing N0/N3

Foundations of group cohomology
H⁰, H¹ and the Six-Term Sequence, fully assembled

We fully assemble from its parts the machine the main part described in one line as "a short exact sequence sprouts an exact sequence". The group structure of Z¹ and B¹, descending continuous cocycles to a finite stage, the well-definedness of the connecting homomorphism δ, and exactness of the six-term sequence at all five places ── verified without skipping a single line.

TEXT: Silverman, AEC 2nd ed. ─ Appendix B §1–§2 (fully expanded)

Main III The Kummer pairing N0 Foundations of cohomology N1 The Kummer sequence N2 Hilbert 90 N3 inf–res and unramified
Section 01

The gaps this note fills

  • ① That the cocycles Z¹ and coboundaries B¹ form groups ── only once this is checked can the quotient H¹ = Z¹/B¹ be defined as a group
  • ② A complete proof that "if the action is trivial, H¹ = Hom(G, M)" (the main part only states the result)
  • ③ The precise meaning of "continuous cocycles" for profinite groups, and a proof that "if continuous, it always factors through some finite Galois extension" ── what's inside the one sentence in the main part, "from now on this convention is implicit"
  • ④ That a G-homomorphism f : M → N induces a map f* on H¹ (what the last two arrows of the exact sequence really are)
  • ⑤ Well-definedness of the connecting homomorphism δ (changing the lift only produces a difference of coboundaries), and that δ is a group homomorphism
  • ⑥ Exactness of the six-term sequence ── verifying it at 5 places. The main part only showed how δ is built and merely stated exactness

This note doesn't use elliptic curves at all. It's purely "assembling the machine". Connecting the machine to elliptic curves is the job of N1, and connecting it to the multiplicative group is the job of N2.

Section 02

Fixing the premises (R1–R3)

Premise R1 ─ G-modules and the right-action convention

Let G be a group and M an abelian group (written additively). M is a G-module if for each σ ∈ G a map m ↦ mσ is given, satisfying:

(i) (m + n)σ = mσ + nσ (each σ is a group homomorphism)
(ii) mστ = (mσ)τ (composition is read as a right action)
(iii) me = m

(ii) is the same convention as premise Q3 in the weak MW notesweakmw N1 premise Q3, matching AEC's exponent notation. From (i)(ii) we also get (−m)σ = −mσ and 0σ = 0 (homomorphisms preserve inverses and the identity).

Premise R2 ─ short exact sequences of G-modules

A sequence of G-modules and G-equivariant homomorphisms (group homomorphisms satisfying f(mσ) = f(m)σ)

0 → A →φ B →ψ C → 0

is a short exact sequence if: φ is injective, ψ is surjective, and im φ = ker ψ. From now on we identify A with a G-submodule of B via its image under φ and write A ⊆ B without comment (then the kernel of ψ is exactly A).

Premise R3 ─ notation

A map c : G → M is written with subscripts, cσ := c(σ). The set of c satisfying the cocycle condition cστ = cστ + cτ is written Z¹(G, M), and the set of those that can be written cσ = mσ − m for some m ∈ M is written B¹(G, M). H⁰(G, M) := MG (the invariant subgroup ── that it's a sub"group" is clear from (i)), H¹(G, M) := Z¹/B¹ (that the quotient can be defined is checked in §03). The topological condition when G is profinite is stated precisely in §04.

Section 03

Rules for computing with cocycles (gaps ①②)

1

Z¹(G, M) is an abelian group under the pointwise sum (c + d)σ := cσ + dσ.

▸ Reason The sum satisfies the cocycle condition: (c+d)στ = cστ + dστ = (cστ + cτ) + (dστ + dτ) = (cσ + dσ)τ + (c+d)τ. The last equality is R1(i). The zero map is a cocycle (0 = 0τ + 0). −c is also a cocycle (flip the sign of both sides and use (−m)τ = −mτ). Associativity and commutativity are inherited pointwise from M.

2

B¹(G, M) ⊆ Z¹(G, M), and B¹ is a subgroup. Therefore the quotient H¹ = Z¹/B¹ can be defined as a group (gap ① resolved).

▸ Reason The coboundary defined by m satisfies the cocycle condition: (mσ − m)τ + (mτ − m) = mστ − mτ + mτ − m = mστ − m. Here we used R1(i)(ii). It's a subgroup: the difference of the coboundary of m and the coboundary of n is the coboundary of m − n.

3

Every cocycle vanishes at the identity: ce = 0.

▸ Reason ce = ce·e = cee + ce = ce + ce (R1(iii)). Subtract ce from both sides.

4

Value at an inverse: cσ⁻¹ = −(cσ)σ⁻¹.

▸ Reason 0 = ce = cσσ⁻¹ = cσσ⁻¹ + cσ⁻¹ (Step 3 and the cocycle condition). Just move the term over. This formula is used in the conjugation lemma of N3.

5

If the action of G is trivial (mσ = m), then H¹(G, M) = Hom(G, M) (gap ② resolved).

▸ Reason The cocycle condition becomes cστ = cστ + cτ = cσ + cτ, equivalent to "c is a group homomorphism G → M". So Z¹ = Hom(G, M). The only coboundary is cσ = mσ − m = m − m = 0, so B¹ = 0. Hence H¹ = Hom(G, M)/0 = Hom(G, M). It's also immediate from the definitions that the group structure on Z¹ (Step 1) agrees with the pointwise sum on Hom.

Section 04

Profinite groups and continuous cocycles (gap ③)

Topological convention (discrete G-modules)

Let G be a profinite group (= an inverse limit of finite groups; it's compact, and the open normal subgroups U form a neighborhood base of the identity), and M an abelian group with the discrete topology. The action is continuous if the stabilizer { σ : mσ = m } of each m ∈ M contains an open subgroup of G. Equivalently: M = ⋃U MU (U running over open normal subgroups). We require cocycles to be continuous as maps c : G → M. GK/K with E[m], E(K), K× all fit this framework (because each point's coordinates lie in a finite extension; verified in N1 and N2).

Coboundaries are automatically continuous: σ ↦ mσ, with U an open subgroup contained in the stabilizer of m, is constant on the cosets Uσ (mησ = (mη)σ = mσ, η ∈ U), i.e. locally constant. The issue is on the cocycle side, and the following is the main theorem of this section.

Theorem (continuous cocycles come from a finite stage) what the implicit convention of AEC App. B §2 contains

Let c ∈ Z¹(G, M) be a continuous cocycle. Then there is an open normal subgroup U such that: c is constant on each coset σU, cη = 0 (∀η ∈ U), all the values cσ lie in MU, and c is the pullback (inflation) of a cocycle c̄ ∈ Z¹(G/U, MU) of the finite group G/U. Therefore

H¹cont(G, M) = ⋃U inf( H¹(G/U, MU) ) (an increasing union, U running over open normal subgroups)
6

A common open normal subgroup U can be taken, and c is constant on each coset σU.

▸ Reason M is discrete, so the single point {cσ} is open. By continuity c⁻¹({cσ}) is an open set containing σ, and from the basic neighborhood system of a profinite group's topology, σVσ ⊆ c⁻¹({cσ}) for some open normal subgroup Vσ. {σVσ}σ∈G is an open cover of G, and G is compact, so there is a finite subcover σ₁V₁, …, σkVk. Let U := V₁ ∩ ⋯ ∩ Vk (a finite intersection of open normal subgroups is open normal). Any σ lies in some σiVi, and σU ⊆ σiViVi = σiVi (Vi is a group). c is constant with value cσᵢ on σiVi, so it's constant on σU too. U is normal, so σU = Uσ (left and right cosets coincide).

7

cη = 0 (∀η ∈ U).

▸ Reason η ∈ U = eU, c is constant on eU, and its value there is ce = 0 (Step 3).

8

All values lie in the invariant part: cσ ∈ MU (∀σ ∈ G).

▸ Reason For η ∈ U, the cocycle condition: cση = cση + cη = cση (Step 7). On the other hand ση ∈ σU, so cση = cσ (Step 6). Hence cση = cσ.

9

c̄σU := cσ defines a well-defined c̄ ∈ Z¹(G/U, MU), and its pullback equals c.

▸ Reason Well-definedness is Step 6 (constant on cosets). Before that, MU is a G/U-module: for m ∈ MU, σ ∈ G, check mσ ∈ MU ── for η ∈ U, (mσ)η = mση = mη′σ (η′ := σησ⁻¹ ∈ U, normality of U) = (mη′)σ = mσ. Moreover, replacing σ by ση in the same class gives mση = (mσ)η = mσ, so the action mσU := mσ doesn't depend on the choice of representative. Descent of the cocycle condition: c̄(σU)(τU) = cστ = cστ + cτ = (c̄σU)τU + c̄τU (the values lie in MU by Step 8). Agreement of the pullback: (inf c̄)σ = c̄σU = cσ, by definition.

∎
? This theorem is the whole of the "implicit convention"

The sentence repeated throughout the main part and the weak MW part, "we only consider cocycles that factor through a finite Galois extension", is precisely the statement of this theorem. The cohomology of a profinite group is nothing but an increasing union of cohomologies of finite groups ── which is why Part 2's "hand computations in the finite group GL/K" and Part 3's "general theory with the absolute Galois group" can go back and forth without contradiction. That inf is injective at the level of classes (so the union increases "without overlap") is a consequence of the inflation–restriction exact sequence in N3.

Section 05

Functoriality ─ constructing f* (gap ④)

10

A G-equivariant homomorphism f : M → N induces a group homomorphism f* : H¹(G, M) → H¹(G, N), [c] ↦ [f∘c]. Likewise on H⁰, f(MG) ⊆ NG.

▸ Reason f∘c is a cocycle: (f∘c)στ = f(cστ + cτ) = f(cσ)τ + f(cτ) (f is additive and G-equivariant). It preserves coboundaries: f(mσ − m) = f(m)σ − f(m), i.e. the coboundary of m goes to the coboundary of f(m). So it's well defined as a map on classes. Being a homomorphism follows pointwise from additivity of f. Continuity: if M is discrete, f∘c is a composition of continuous maps (every map from a discrete space is continuous). On the H⁰ side: mσ = m ⇒ f(m)σ = f(mσ) = f(m).

Section 06

The connecting homomorphism δ (gap ⑤)

Let 0 → A → B →ψ C → 0 be a short exact sequence (R2), and take c ∈ CG. We break the construction the main part described in three lines into things to verify.

11

Construction: choose one lift b ∈ B (ψ(b) = c) and set dσ := bσ − b. Then dσ ∈ A.

▸ Reason A lift exists by surjectivity of ψ (R2). ψ(dσ) = ψ(bσ) − ψ(b) = ψ(b)σ − ψ(b) = cσ − c = 0 (G-equivariance of ψ and c ∈ CG). So dσ ∈ ker ψ = A (R2).

12

d is a cocycle with values in A: d ∈ Z¹(G, A).

▸ Reason bστ − b = (bσ − b)τ + (bτ − b) (the same identity as Step 2, R1(i)(ii)). All values lie in A (Step 11), and A is a G-submodule, so this can be read as an equation inside A. Continuity: σ ↦ bσ is locally constant (the same argument as for coboundaries at the start of §04), so d is continuous.

13

Well-definedness: choosing another lift b′ changes d only by a coboundary of A. Hence δ(c) := [d] ∈ H¹(G, A) doesn't depend on the lift (gap ⑤ resolved).

▸ Reason ψ(b′) = ψ(b) = c gives a := b′ − b ∈ ker ψ = A. d′σ − dσ = (b′σ − b′) − (bσ − b) = aσ − a. Since a ∈ A, this is an element of B¹(G, A).

14

δ : CG → H¹(G, A) is a group homomorphism.

▸ Reason Choose lifts b₁, b₂ of c₁, c₂ ∈ CG; then b₁ + b₂ is a lift of c₁ + c₂ (ψ is a homomorphism). Computing with this choice, (b₁+b₂)σ − (b₁+b₂) = d₁σ + d₂σ. By Step 13 the value of δ doesn't depend on the choice of lift, so δ(c₁+c₂) = [d₁] + [d₂] = δ(c₁) + δ(c₂).

Section 07 ─ Main

The six-term sequence ─ exactness at 5 places (gap ⑥)

Theorem (the low-degree long exact sequence) AEC App. B §2

If 0 → A → B →ψ C → 0 is a short exact sequence of G-modules, the following is exact:

0 → AG →φ BG →ψ CG →δ H¹(G,A) →φ* H¹(G,B) →ψ* H¹(G,C)

(It holds just as well in the setting of profinite groups, discrete modules and continuous cocycles. A note on continuity is added to the reason for each step.)

Figure 1: the six-term sequence. There are 5 places to check exactness (we make no claim at the right end H¹(G,C) ── measuring the "continuation" there is H², which these notes don't need).
15

Exact at AG (= injectivity of φ|AG). Also check φ(AG) ⊆ BG and ψ(BG) ⊆ CG.

▸ Reason φ is injective on all of A (R2), so it's injective on the subset AG too. Checking where things land: if a ∈ AG, then φ(a)σ = φ(aσ) = φ(a), i.e. φ(a) ∈ BG (G-equivariance of φ). Likewise for ψ. ── Only after this check does "a sequence between invariant parts" make sense.

16

Exact at BG: ker(ψ|BG) = φ(AG).

▸ Reason (⊇) ψ∘φ = 0 is one half of im φ = ker ψ (R2). (⊆) If b ∈ BG and ψ(b) = 0, then b ∈ ker ψ = im φ, i.e. b = φ(a) for some a ∈ A. a is invariant: φ(aσ) = φ(a)σ = bσ = b = φ(a), and injectivity of φ gives aσ = a. So b ∈ φ(AG).

17

Exact at CG: ker δ = ψ(BG).

▸ Reason (⊇) If c = ψ(b) with b ∈ BG, we can choose b itself as the lift (Step 13 guarantees the freedom of choice), and dσ = bσ − b = 0. So δ(c) = 0. (⊆) If δ(c) = 0, then for a lift b, bσ − b = aσ − a (for some a ∈ A, by the definition of coboundary). Setting b′ := b − a, b′σ − b′ = (bσ − b) − (aσ − a) = 0, i.e. b′ ∈ BG. Moreover ψ(b′) = ψ(b) − ψ(a) = c − 0 = c (a ∈ A = ker ψ). So c ∈ ψ(BG).

18

Exact at H¹(G, A): ker φ* = im δ.

▸ Reason (⊇) φ*δ(c) is "the class of σ ↦ bσ − b read inside B", which is exactly the coboundary of b ∈ B. So it's 0. (⊆) Suppose [c] ∈ H¹(G, A) satisfies φ*[c] = 0. It's a coboundary in B, i.e. cσ = bσ − b (∀σ) for some b ∈ B. Setting y := ψ(b) ∈ C, yσ − y = ψ(bσ − b) = ψ(cσ) = 0 (cσ ∈ A = ker ψ), so y ∈ CG. Computing δ of this y with the lift b gives δ(y) = [σ ↦ bσ − b] = [c]. So [c] ∈ im δ.

19

Exact at H¹(G, B): ker ψ* = im φ*.

▸ Reason (⊇) ψ*φ*[c] = [(ψ∘φ)∘c] = [0] (ψ∘φ = 0). (⊆) Suppose [d] ∈ H¹(G, B) satisfies ψ*[d] = 0. It's a coboundary in C, i.e. ψ(dσ) = yσ − y (∀σ) for some y ∈ C. By surjectivity of ψ choose a lift b₀ ∈ B of y, and set d′σ := dσ − (b₀σ − b₀). d′ is cohomologous to d (they differ by the coboundary of b₀). ψ(d′σ) = (yσ − y) − (ψ(b₀)σ − ψ(b₀)) = (yσ − y) − (yσ − y) = 0, so all values of d′ lie in A = ker ψ. d′ is a cocycle of B with values in A, hence a cocycle of A (the same argument as Step 12). Continuity: σ ↦ b₀σ is locally constant, so d′ is continuous too. So [d] = [d′] = φ*[d′] ∈ im φ*.

∎ (all 5 places checked)
? Why δ is involved in exactness "from both sides"

Lining up the checks, δ appears as "the kernel on the left" in Step 17 and as "the image on the right" in Step 18. That is, δ is a bridge identifying "invariant elements that don't come from BG" with "cohomology classes that vanish in B". The precise content of the main part's slogan, "δ measures the obstruction to division", is nothing but these two exactness statements combined.
